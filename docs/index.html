What Calculus Lab is

Calculus Lab is a free, interactive visualizer for the two central ideas of Calculus 1: the derivative and the integral. It is built for students who are seeing these concepts for the first time. Instead of a static textbook figure, it animates the mechanism — the tangent line moving along a curve, its slope being plotted as a second graph, rectangles filling the area under a curve — and works every example out by hand, step by step, naming the rule used at each step.

How to use it

  1. Choose Derivatives or Integrals in the navigation bar.
  2. Pick a preset function (x², x³ − 3x, sin(x), cos(x), eˣ, ln(x), 1/x, x·sin(x), sin(x²), √x) or type your own.
  3. Press Play, or scrub the slider to move through the animation by hand.
  4. Follow the three-part walk-through on the right: what you're finding, how to do it by hand, and how it maps onto the graph right now.
  5. Expand the rules toolbox for a quick reference to the power, product, quotient, chain, and sum rules (and their integral counterparts).

Common calculus questions

What is a derivative?

The derivative of a function is its instantaneous rate of change: the slope of the line that just touches the curve at a single point (the tangent line). It is written f′(x) or dy/dx. The collection of those slopes, one for every x, is itself a function — the derivative function.

What is an integral?

A definite integral is the signed area between a curve and the x-axis over an interval: area above the axis counts as positive, area below counts as negative. An indefinite integral is the family of all antiderivatives of a function.

What is the power rule?

The power rule: the derivative of xn is n·xn−1. Bring the exponent down as a multiplier, then subtract one from the exponent. So the derivative of x³ is 3x².

How do you differentiate a product like x·sin(x)?

Use the product rule: (A·B)′ = A′·B + A·B′. With A = x (so A′ = 1) and B = sin(x) (so B′ = cos(x)), the derivative is (1)·sin(x) + x·cos(x) = sin(x) + x·cos(x).

What is the chain rule?

The chain rule handles a function nested inside another. For f(g(x)), the derivative is f′(g(x))·g′(x): differentiate the outer function, leave the inside alone, then multiply by the derivative of the inside. Example: d/dx[sin(x²)] = cos(x²)·2x.

What is a Riemann sum?

A Riemann sum estimates the area under a curve using thin rectangles. As the rectangles get thinner and more numerous, the sum approaches the exact definite integral.

What is the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus?

It links differentiation and integration: to evaluate a definite integral of f from a to b exactly, find an antiderivative F and compute F(b) − F(a).
